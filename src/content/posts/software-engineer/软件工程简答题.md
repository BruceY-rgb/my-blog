**Explain how size-oriented metrics differ from function-oriented metrics. Discuss the pros and cons of each. Why is it important for software developers to make use of measurement to guide their work?**

Size-oriented metrics are computed by normalizing **direct measures** of the software engineering process, such as effort, cost or defects, by the size of the product, usually measured in lines of code.

Function-oriented metrics are indirect measures that are computed from **information-domain values** of a business application and an assessment of its complexity, such as function points.

Size-oriented metrics are e**asy to collect and understand**, but they **depend on programming language** and are difficult to apply to component-based or visual programming environments.

Function-oriented metrics can be **estimated earlier in the software life cycle and are relatively language-independent**, but they are **more abstract and may involve subjective judgment**.

Software developers need measurement because without measurement **it is difficult to know whether process quality**, **product quality**, **productivity or project performance** is improving.
